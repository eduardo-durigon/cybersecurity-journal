One of the biggest challenges with CompTIA questions is that several answers can sometimes appear technically possible.

The objective is often not simply to identify something that could solve the problem, but to determine the **best or most appropriate next step** based on the scenario.

Because of this, I have been paying particular attention to troubleshooting methodology and understanding when specific tools should be used.

Some of the Windows tools and concepts I reviewed included:

* `netstat`
* `msconfig`

* Kerberos authentication

This process has helped expose smaller gaps in my knowledge that were less obvious while progressing through the individual course modules.

---

## Key Takeaways

The biggest takeaway this week was the difference between **recognizing a concept and being able to apply it correctly in a scenario**.

During the course, topics are usually studied individually.

Practice exams are different.

A question may combine troubleshooting methodology, Windows tools, networking, security, and operational procedures into a single scenario.

This requires understanding not only what a tool does, but also **when and why it should be used**.

My current revision process is:

**Practice Question → Identify Mistake → Review Concept → Understand Reasoning → Try Again**

This has been much more useful than simply memorizing correct answers.

Every incorrect answer identifies something that can be improved before the real exam.
