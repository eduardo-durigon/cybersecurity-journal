# Weekly Cybersecurity Journal – Week 36

## Overview

This week continued the final preparation phase for the **CompTIA A+ Core 2 exam**.

Most of my study time was spent completing practice tests, working through labs, and revisiting topics where I still need more confidence.

At this stage, the preparation process is becoming increasingly focused.

Instead of moving on to new topics, I am concentrating on identifying gaps in my knowledge, reviewing incorrect answers, and reinforcing what I have already learned.

The goal is to make sure I can apply the material consistently before sitting the exam.

---

## Exam Preparation

Practice exams remained the main focus this week.

The questions continue to cover material from across Core 2, particularly:

* Windows troubleshooting
* Administrative tools
* Malware and security
* Authentication
* System performance
* Storage
* Mobile devices
* Networking
* Operational procedures

One area I have continued working on is understanding why an answer is correct rather than simply memorizing it.

Practice questions often require choosing between tools or procedures that appear similar but serve different purposes.

Knowing the definition of each tool is not always enough.

The important skill is being able to look at a problem and determine **which tool is appropriate for that particular situation**.

Reviewing incorrect answers helps identify where my understanding is incomplete and what needs more attention.

---

## Labs and Knowledge Review

Alongside exam preparation, I continued working through labs and revisiting areas where I need more confidence.

The aim is to reinforce the concepts covered during my studies and become more comfortable applying them in practical situations.

Practice tests help identify weaknesses, while labs provide an opportunity to work with the tools and procedures rather than only answering questions about them.

I am also trying to connect related concepts instead of treating every topic as an isolated piece of information.

In real IT support environments, troubleshooting requires understanding the symptoms, considering possible causes, selecting the appropriate tools, and checking whether the solution works.

This is the kind of approach I want to keep developing as I prepare for the exam and for the practical work that will follow.

---

## Key Takeaways

The biggest takeaway this week is that progress does not always mean learning something new.

Sometimes, the most useful thing to do is revisit familiar material, identify weaknesses, and reinforce what has already been learned.

Practice tests and labs serve different purposes, but both help turn knowledge into something more reliable.

At this stage, I want to focus on consistency and understanding rather than rushing through the remaining preparation.

The objective is not just to recognize the right answer during an exam, but to build a foundation I can use when troubleshooting real problems.

For now, the priority remains finishing what I started.
