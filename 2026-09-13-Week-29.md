## Module 20 – Supporting Mobile Software

Module 20 focused on supporting and securing mobile operating systems, particularly **Android and iOS**.

Some of the main topics included:

* Screen locks and authentication
* Biometrics and two-factor authentication
* Device encryption
* Mobile OS updates
* BYOD and CYOD
* Mobile Device Management (MDM)
* Location services and remote wipe
* Application permissions and sideloading
* Rooting and jailbreaking
* Mobile malware and phishing
* Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and NFC troubleshooting
* Mobile OS and application troubleshooting

One of my main takeaways was that smartphones and tablets should be treated as **full endpoints rather than simply personal devices**.

A phone can contain email, passwords, authentication applications, VPN access, business information, cloud accounts, and other sensitive data. Because mobile devices are also easily lost or stolen, controls such as encryption, strong authentication, location services, MDM, and remote wipe become particularly important.

The troubleshooting process also continued the same principle I have seen throughout A+:

**Start with the simplest and least destructive solution first.**

## Module 21 – Using Data Security

Module 21 focused on protecting information throughout its lifecycle.

The main areas included:

* Full, incremental, and differential backups
* Backup chains and rotation schemes
* Grandfather-Father-Son (GFS)
* The 3-2-1 backup rule
* Backup testing and recovery
* Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
* Data Loss Prevention (DLP)
* Data retention
* Software licensing
* Incident response
* Digital forensics
* Order of volatility
* Chain of custody
* Secure data destruction
* AI security, privacy, bias, and hallucinations

The backup material reinforced an important point:

**A backup is only useful if the data can actually be recovered.**

Creating backups is not enough. They need to be verified and recovery procedures need to be tested.

I also found the digital forensics section interesting, particularly the importance of preserving evidence and following the **order of volatility**:

**CPU/cache → RAM → temporary data → disk → archival media**

The most volatile information needs to be collected first because it is the most likely to disappear.

The section on AI was also relevant because AI is increasingly becoming part of normal IT environments. AI can be extremely useful, but organizations also need to consider **accuracy, hallucinations, bias, privacy, and what information employees are allowed to provide to AI systems**.

## Key Takeaways

The biggest theme this week was **protecting information regardless of where it exists**.

On mobile devices, that means authentication, encryption, application security, MDM, and the ability to protect a device if it is lost or stolen.

For organizational data, protection extends across the entire lifecycle:

**Store → Protect → Back up → Recover → Retain → Investigate → Destroy**

The incident response and digital forensics material also reinforced that security is not only about preventing an incident. IT professionals need to know what to do **after something goes wrong**, while avoiding actions that could make the situation worse or destroy evidence.
